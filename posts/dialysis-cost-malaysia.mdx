## Dialysis Costs: What Families Need to Know

Hemodialysis is usually done **3 times a week, for life**. That means a recurring cost every single week — and without help, it can strain any family's finances.

The good news: **most patients in Malaysia do not pay the full cost.** Several aid channels can reduce the bill, and some offer completely free treatment to eligible patients.

According to the National Kidney Foundation (NKF) of Malaysia, one hemodialysis treatment at their centers costs **around RM170 per session**. At 13 sessions a month, the full cost can exceed RM2,000 monthly — which is exactly why financial aid matters so much.

## Comparing Types of Centers

| Type of center | Cost to the patient |
| --- | --- |
| **Government hospital (MOH)** | Heavily subsidised for eligible citizens, but slots are limited and there may be a waiting list |
| **NGO centers (e.g. NKF)** | Treatment costs around RM170 per session (source: NKF); patients pay far less after the MOH subsidy and welfare support |
| **Zakat centers (e.g. PHZ Penang)** | Free for eligible Muslim patients |
| **Private centers** | Rates are set by each center and vary — contact the center for a quote |

The government is also working to ease this burden. According to a Berita RTM report (September 2025), the Ministry of Health is implementing a **long-term plan to control dialysis costs for B40 patients**.

## Financial Aid Options, One by One

### 1. MOH Subsidy — RM100 per treatment

According to NKF, eligible patients receiving treatment at NGO dialysis centers can get a **Ministry of Health subsidy of RM100 per treatment**. The application is usually handled through the dialysis center itself — ask the staff when registering.

### 2. NKF Patient Welfare

NKF Malaysia runs dialysis centers with subsidised rates. Self-paying patients are charged **RM90 per session**, and NKF covers the remaining cost through its welfare fund for eligible patients after an assessment. Learn more at the [NKF patient welfare page](https://nkf.org.my/patient-welfare/).

### 3. State Zakat Bodies

Every state has a zakat body offering medical aid, including dialysis costs. A leading example is the **Pusat Hemodialisis Zakat (PHZ)** run by Zakat Pulau Pinang.

According to a Zakat MAINPP TV video, PHZ provides **free hemodialysis treatment to eligible Muslim patients**, with eight branches across Penang treating over 200 patients. The annual cost of around RM6 million is fully covered by zakat funds.

If you are in another state, contact your state zakat body (e.g. Lembaga Zakat Selangor, MAIWP, Zakat Kedah) and ask about medical or dialysis assistance.

### 4. KWAP — For Government Pensioners

Government pensioners can apply for **dialysis treatment financing through KWAP**. The KWAP Sayang Pesara channel has published an application guide video — see the link in the References section.

### 5. SOCSO (PERKESO)

Eligible SOCSO contributors may qualify for dialysis treatment benefits at approved centers. Eligibility depends on your contribution scheme — check directly with your nearest [PERKESO office](https://www.perkeso.gov.my).

### 6. Insurance and Employers

Check the patient's medical insurance or takaful policy — some policies cover dialysis costs. If the patient is still working, ask the employer about company medical benefits.

## Practical Steps to Apply

1. **Get a medical report and confirmation letter** from the treating doctor or hospital.
2. **Prepare basic documents**: identity card, proof of income (payslip or confirmation of no income), and treatment bills if available.
3. **Visit the Medical Social Work Department** at a government hospital — social work officers can refer you to suitable aid.
4. **Ask directly at the dialysis center** — NGO and zakat centers usually handle subsidy applications on the patient's behalf.
5. **Apply through more than one channel** — zakat aid, the MOH subsidy and NGO welfare can be combined in most cases.
