## Introduction

When someone we love is diagnosed with kidney failure, the first response is usually not logic — it is hope. We want to believe there is an easier way: one product, one remedy, one "cure" that avoids dialysis.

That feeling is normal, and no one should be shamed for it. Unfortunately, this hope is exactly what irresponsible product sellers exploit.

In her kidney myth video series, Dr Rafidah Abdullah, a Malaysian consultant nephrologist, answers the most common myths one by one. This article summarises her explanations.

## Myth 1: "There are products that can cure damaged kidneys"

**What people believe:** On Facebook, TikTok and WhatsApp, countless products claim to "restore" the kidneys — complete with testimonials from patients who supposedly recovered and no longer need dialysis.

**What Dr Rafidah explains:** In her "Produk SAMPAH" (junk products) episode, she points out that under Malaysia's Medicines (Advertisement and Sale) Act 1956, product advertisements are not allowed to claim to treat kidney disease. Any product claiming to cure kidneys in its advertising is already breaking the law.

She also explains:

- Testimonials are not evidence. "Cured" stories cannot be verified and are often fabricated to drive sales.
- Existing studies do not show that such products restore kidney function.
- Some supplement ingredients can actually make kidney function **worse**.
- If you or your family still want to try something, bring the product to the treating doctor **before** taking it — not after.

**The risk:** Thousands of ringgit wasted, real treatment delayed, and remaining kidney function damaged faster.

### How to check if a product is registered with KKM

All legitimate medicines and supplements in Malaysia must be registered with the Ministry of Health (KKM). Before buying:

1. Look for the MAL registration number and Meditag hologram sticker on the packaging.
2. Verify the product's registration status with NPRA/KKM (the National Pharmaceutical Regulatory Agency) through their official website or KKM hotline.
3. Remember: **registered does not mean proven to cure.** Registration only means the product is safe to take as labelled — it still cannot treat kidney failure.

## Myth 2: "Shrunken kidneys can recover"

**What people believe:** Viral videos claim that kidney failure at any stage — including end-stage or "buah pinggang kering" (shrunken kidneys) — can fully recover with certain products or practices.

**What Dr Rafidah explains:** In episode 16, she addresses this claim directly: it is not true. Stage 5 (end-stage) chronic kidney failure cannot return to normal. Kidneys that have shrunk and scarred cannot be "revived" by any product.

What can be done is to protect the function that remains — control blood pressure and sugar, manage salt, potassium and phosphate intake, and follow your doctor's advice. When function drops too low, the proven treatments are dialysis or a kidney transplant.

**The risk:** Patients who believe this myth often delay or stop dialysis. This can become life-threatening within a short time.

## Myth 3: "Virgin coconut oil treats kidney disease"

**What people believe:** Virgin coconut oil (VCO), red palm oil or olive oil are promoted as natural remedies that can heal the kidneys.

**What Dr Rafidah explains:** There is no evidence that virgin coconut oil or any oil can treat kidney disease. In fact, kidney patients are already at high risk of heart disease — adding saturated fat to the diet for no reason is not a wise move. She advises against wasting money on expensive oil products sold on baseless claims.

**The risk:** Beyond wasted money, patients may neglect the real dietary guidance that matters for their kidneys and heart.

## Myth 4: "Donating a kidney makes the donor sick"

**What people believe:** Many are afraid to become donors because they believe they will develop kidney disease themselves after donating.

**What Dr Rafidah explains:** In her transplant myths session, she explains that humans are born with two kidneys and can live healthily with just one. Donors go through thorough medical screening — scans, kidney function tests and counselling — to make sure they are truly healthy and donating voluntarily before being allowed to proceed.

**The risk:** This unfounded fear is one reason Malaysia has among the lowest organ donation rates in the world — and many patients stay on dialysis when a transplant is the best treatment for kidney failure.

## Myth 5: "Life can never be normal after a transplant"

**What people believe:** Transplant recipients supposedly cannot work, exercise or get pregnant after surgery.

**What Dr Rafidah explains:** This is a myth. After a successful transplant, recipients are no longer tied to dialysis sessions three times a week. They can work, become active again, and women can plan pregnancy with their doctor's monitoring. The risk of organ rejection exists but is low with immunosuppressant medication, and it decreases year by year.

She also explains that in Malaysia, organ donation must be voluntary — kidneys cannot be bought or sold. Transplant costs at government hospitals are subsidised, far lower than most people assume.

**The risk:** Patients who believe this myth turn down the chance of a transplant — the treatment that offers the best quality of life and life expectancy.

## Why these myths are dangerous

Three losses repeat across every myth above:

1. **Delayed treatment** — time that should be spent planning dialysis or a transplant is lost.
2. **Wasted money** — these products are not cheap, and the only ones who profit are the sellers.
3. **More damage** — some products genuinely harm the kidney function that remains.

Product sellers know that patients' families are searching for hope. Do not let that hope be traded on.

## What you can do

- Talk to a nephrologist or your treating doctor before trying any product or supplement.
- Check product registration with NPRA/KKM — and remember registration is not proof of a cure.
- Never stop dialysis or prescribed medication without your doctor's advice.
- If family members share "miracle" products, respond gently — they want the best too. Share Dr Rafidah's videos as a reference.
